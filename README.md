# Against Molinism: A Theological Indictment

## Abstract

This paper presents a theological and philosophical indictment of Molinism, focusing on its central charge that Compatibilism makes God the author of evil. Through rigorous exegetical analysis and logical critique, we demonstrate that this accusation not only lacks scriptural warrant but is logically incoherent and ultimately reverses upon Molinism itself. The argument proceeds through six stages: (1) steel-manning the Molinist critique at full strength, (2) exposing the structural inversion in Molinist argumentation, which assumes libertarian freedom without biblical justification, (3) demonstrating from Scripture that biblical responsibility never requires libertarian freedom and that counterfactuals do not necessitate middle knowledge, (4) revealing the logical incoherence of Molinism's own position regarding divine causation, including internal critiques from Molinist philosophers themselves, (5) presenting the positive case for why Compatibilism does not make God the author of evil, and (6) demonstrating that the god of Molinism is a philosophically constructed idol fundamentally incompatible with the YHWH of Scripture. We conclude that Molinism sacrifices biblical sovereignty on the altar of philosophical intuitions about freedom, constructing a composite, dependent deity whose purposes can be thwarted by creaturely choice. This is not theological refinement but idolatry.

## 1. Introduction

Molinism has gained considerable traction in contemporary evangelical theology, largely through the work of William Lane Craig and other Christian philosophers. Central to its apologetic appeal is the charge that Compatibilism (the view that divine sovereignty and human responsibility are compatible within a framework of exhaustive divine decree) makes God the author of evil and therefore morally culpable for sin. This paper will demonstrate that this charge is not only exegetically unfounded but logically incoherent, and that Molinism itself, far from preserving biblical fidelity, constructs a philosophical substitute for the God of Scripture, an idol fashioned to fit human intuitions about autonomy and justice.

The argument proceeds in six stages: First, we steel-man the Molinist critique to engage it at full strength. Second, we expose the structural inversion in their argumentation, the assumption of libertarian freedom as the default position without scriptural warrant. Third, we demonstrate from Scripture itself that biblical responsibility never requires libertarian freedom and that counterfactuals do not necessitate middle knowledge. Fourth, we present the positive case for why Compatibilism does not make God the author of evil. Finally, we demonstrate that the god of Molinism is not the biblical YHWH but a philosophically constructed idol that must be rejected.

## 2. The Molinist Critique (Steel-manned)

To engage the strongest form of the argument, we turn to William Lane Craig's formulation (particularly in *The Only Wise God* and *Divine Foreknowledge and Human Freedom*), along with Thomas Flint's *Divine Providence: The Molinist Account*. These works represent the most sophisticated philosophical defense of Molinism and its critique of Compatibilism.

### The Core Argument

1. If determinism is true, God is the ultimate cause of all human choices, including evil ones
2. If God is the ultimate cause of evil choices, He is morally responsible for them
3. Moral responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise (Principle of Alternate Possibilities)
4. Therefore, Compatibilism makes God the author of sin

### Craig's Supporting Claims

**The manipulation argument**: If God determines your desires and then you act on them, you are functionally equivalent to a puppet. The real agent is the puppeteer. No amount of internal desire-satisfaction can create genuine agency if those desires themselves are determined by an external force. The puppet may "want" to move as it moves, but this doesn't make it the real author of its movements.

**The sourcehood objection**: Moral responsibility requires being the ultimate source of your actions. If God is the ultimate source (determining not just circumstances but the internal states that guarantee certain responses), then responsibility cannot be genuinely transferred to the creature. The buck must stop somewhere, and in Compatibilism, it stops with God.

**The "bringing about" problem**: Compatibilism doesn't merely have God permitting evil (standing back and allowing what He could prevent). It has God actively bringing evil about by determining the circumstances and internal states that guarantee its occurrence. This makes God's relationship to evil active, not passive. He is the architect of evil acts, even if He uses secondary causes.

**Biblical language**: Scripture's pervasive use of divine grief (Genesis 6:6), testing (Genesis 22:1), conditional statements ("if you obey..."), and commands that presuppose genuine choice all imply that God does not determine all outcomes. The emotional and relational categories Scripture uses to describe God's interaction with humans make sense only if human choices possess libertarian freedom.

### The Molinist Alternative

Middle knowledge provides the solution. God possesses three logical moments of knowledge:

1. **Natural knowledge**: What could be (all possibilities)
2. **Middle knowledge**: What would be (what free creatures would do in any set of circumstances)
3. **Free knowledge**: What will be (what God actualizes based on His decree)

Middle knowledge is the crucial innovation. It is God's knowledge of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom, true propositions about what free creatures would do in circumstances that may never actualize. For example, God knows that if Peter were placed in the courtyard during Jesus's trial, he would freely deny Christ three times. This knowledge is logically prior to God's creative decree.

Armed with middle knowledge, God surveys all feasible worlds (worlds He could actualize given what creatures would freely do) and selects which one to actualize. He achieves His purposes not through causal determination but through providential arrangement, placing free creatures in circumstances where He knows what they will freely choose.

This preserves both sovereignty (God chooses which feasible world to actualize, achieving His purposes) and freedom (creatures retain libertarian choice, the ability to do otherwise in identical circumstances). Evil occurs because creatures genuinely choose it, not because God determined them to choose it. God's foreknowledge of what they would do doesn't causally determine their choice; He simply knows what they would freely do and decides whether to actualize those circumstances.

Craig argues this is the only system that maintains both divine sovereignty and creaturely freedom without making God the author of evil. Compatibilism, he claims, collapses into mere determinism where human "freedom" is just doing what you want, but your wants are ultimately determined by God, making you His instrument rather than a genuine agent.

## 3. The Structural Inversion: Who Bears the Burden?

The Molinist argument commits a fundamental category error at its foundation: **it assumes libertarian freedom is the biblical default and demands Compatibilists prove their position doesn't violate it.** This inverts the burden of proof entirely.

### The Actual Burden of Proof

Consider what Molinism requires for its critique to succeed:

- Molinism introduces libertarian freedom (defined as the ability to do otherwise in identical circumstances with identical internal states) as necessary for moral responsibility
- This specific concept appears nowhere in Scripture's explicit teaching on human agency, culpability, or divine judgment
- Molinism then argues that Compatibilism violates this requirement
- Therefore, **Molinists must first demonstrate from Scripture that libertarian freedom is required** before they can charge Compatibilism with making God evil's author

**They never do this exegetical work.** Instead, the argumentative pattern runs:

1. Assume libertarian freedom is intuitive, self-evident, or philosophically necessary
2. Show that Compatibilism is incompatible with libertarian freedom
3. Conclude that Compatibilism therefore fails biblically and morally

But step 1 is an enormous unargued philosophical premise masquerading as common sense. The question is not "Does Compatibilism fit libertarian freedom?" but "Does Scripture require libertarian freedom for moral responsibility?" If it doesn't, the entire critique is a category error, like rejecting quantum mechanics because it violates Newtonian intuitions.

### The Pattern of Philosophical Imposition

This is not unique to Molinism; it follows a well-worn path in Christian theology where extrabiblical philosophical categories are imposed on Scripture and then used to judge competing views. Consider the parallel:

- **Open Theism** assumes exhaustive divine foreknowledge is incompatible with genuine human freedom, imports process philosophy's categories, and then judges classical theism as making humans into robots
- **Arminianism** assumes prevenient grace must be resistible or else humans aren't truly free, and then judges Calvinism as violating human agency
- **Molinism** assumes libertarian freedom is necessary for responsibility, and then judges Compatibilism as making God the author of evil

In each case, the pattern is identical:
1. Import a philosophical framework
2. Make it the unquestioned standard
3. Judge Scripture's clear teachings against it
4. Modify or reject what doesn't fit

The proper method is the reverse:
1. Examine what Scripture actually teaches about the matter
2. Submit philosophical intuitions to scriptural authority
3. Affirm what Scripture affirms, even if philosophically puzzling
4. Trust divine revelation over human reason

### Who Must Prove What?

The burden of proof structure should be:

**Molinists must demonstrate:**
- That Scripture requires libertarian freedom for moral responsibility
- That biblical counterfactuals necessitate middle knowledge as the grounding
- That divine sovereignty in Scripture is limited by libertarian constraints
- That the biblical authors operated with these philosophical categories

**Compatibilists must demonstrate:**
- That Scripture teaches exhaustive divine sovereignty
- That Scripture holds humans responsible without requiring libertarian freedom
- That divine decree and human agency are both affirmed in Scripture
- That responsibility is grounded in biblical categories (intent, character, willing action), not philosophical ones (ultimate sourcehood, ability to do otherwise)

The asymmetry is crucial. Compatibilism claims to simply affirm what Scripture clearly and repeatedly teaches, even if the mechanism is mysterious. Molinism claims Scripture requires a specific philosophical framework not found in the text itself. The burden falls on those making the claim requiring extrabiblical categories.

**Molinists have never met this burden.** They assume libertarian freedom, show it's incompatible with Compatibilism, and conclude Compatibilism is unbiblical. But the assumption is never proven from Scripture. It's simply taken as axiomatic, which is precisely the error we must expose.

## 4. The Exegetical Case: Scripture's Actual Teaching on Agency and Culpability

When we turn from philosophical assumptions to scriptural data, a consistent pattern emerges: **biblical responsibility never requires libertarian freedom, and counterfactuals do not necessitate middle knowledge.** Scripture holds humans fully accountable based on intent, character, and willing action, never based on the ability to do otherwise in identical circumstances.

### A. Biblical Responsibility Never Requires Libertarian Freedom

Scripture presents a consistent pattern across every major text addressing divine sovereignty and human culpability. Before examining individual passages, note the pattern that emerges in **every case**:

1. **Divine sovereignty is exhaustive**: God determines, predestines, hardens, works the willing, establishes the steps
2. **Human responsibility is absolute**: No mitigation, no excuse, full culpability assigned
3. **No libertarian freedom invoked**: Scripture never appeals to "could have done otherwise" as the ground of responsibility
4. **Responsibility grounded in biblical categories**: Intent (what they meant), character (evil heart), willing action (they chose according to their nature)
5. **No explanation of mechanism**: Scripture asserts both divine decree and human responsibility without philosophical resolution

This pattern appears in **every relevant text**. Examine the evidence:

#### Genesis 50:20: Joseph and His Brothers

> "As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to bring it about that many people should be kept alive, as they are today."

- Joseph's brothers are held fully responsible for evil intent
- God simultaneously meant the same events for good purposes
- Both divine purpose and human culpability are real
- No suggestion their ability to "do otherwise" was necessary for their guilt
- Their guilt stems from their evil intent and murderous desires, period
- No tension is presented between divine purposing and human responsibility

#### Acts 2:23 and 4:27-28: The Crucifixion

> "This Jesus, delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God, you crucified and killed by the hands of lawless men." (2:23)

> "For truly in this city there were gathered together against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, along with the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel, to do whatever your hand and your plan had predestined to take place." (4:27-28)

- The most wicked act in history (deicide) was predestined by God's definite plan
- Yet all participants (Herod, Pilate, Gentiles, Israel) are held morally accountable
- The text presents **no tension whatsoever** between divine predestination and human guilt
- Luke doesn't say "predestined to allow" or "foresaw and permitted." He says they did "whatever your hand and your plan had predestined to take place"
- Responsibility is grounded in their hostile desires and murderous intent, not in their ability to choose otherwise
- If any event should create the "author of evil" problem, it's this one, yet Scripture has no hesitation declaring both divine predestination and human guilt

#### Exodus 4:21, 7:3, 9:12, 10:1, 10:20, 10:27, 11:10, 14:4: Pharaoh's Hardening

> "The LORD said to Moses, 'When you go back to Egypt, see that you do before Pharaoh all the miracles that I have put in your power. But I will harden his heart, so that he will not let the people go.'" (4:21)

> "But the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he did not listen to them, as the LORD had spoken to Moses." (9:12)

- God explicitly hardens Pharaoh's heart. This is stated eight times across the narrative
- Pharaoh is judged for actions that result from divine hardening
- The text never suggests this creates a moral problem requiring resolution
- Pharaoh's guilt is his own stubbornness, which God judicially hardens further
- No libertarian freedom is invoked to preserve moral responsibility
- God's purposeful hardening and Pharaoh's culpability coexist without explanation

#### 2 Thessalonians 2:11-12: God Sends Delusion

> "Therefore God sends them a strong delusion, so that they may believe what is false, in order that all may be condemned who did not believe the truth but had pleasure in unrighteousness."

- God actively sends (πέμπει) delusion, not merely permits it
- The purpose is explicit: "so that they may believe what is false"
- The result is judicial: "in order that all may be condemned"
- Yet those condemned are responsible. They "did not believe the truth but had pleasure in unrighteousness"
- Their guilt is grounded in their love of unrighteousness, not in God's sending delusion
- God's active role in their deception serves His judicial purposes
- Paul presents no tension between divine sending and human culpability

#### 1 Kings 22:19-23 / 2 Chronicles 18:18-22: Ahab's Decreed Death

> "Now therefore behold, the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these your prophets; the LORD has declared disaster for you... So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat the king of Judah went up to Ramoth-gilead. And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, 'I will disguise myself and go into battle, but you wear your robes.'... But a certain man drew his bow at random and struck the king of Israel between the scale armor and the breastplate... So the king died." (1 Kings 22:23, 29-30, 34, 37)

- God actively sends a lying spirit to deceive Ahab's prophets
- Ahab makes his own strategic choices (disguises himself to avoid targeting)
- An archer shoots "at random" with no knowledge of Ahab's identity
- The arrow hits the one vulnerable spot between armor plates
- Ahab dies exactly as God had decreed through Micaiah's prophecy
- Multiple layers of human agency (prophets lying, Ahab's decision, random arrow) all converge to accomplish God's predetermined outcome
- No suggestion that God's orchestration of deception creates moral culpability for God
- The false prophets and Ahab are responsible for their choices; God uses their willing deception and Ahab's pride to accomplish His judgment

#### Proverbs 16:4, 9 and 21:1: Divine Sovereignty Over Hearts

> "The LORD has made everything for its purpose, even the wicked for the day of trouble." (16:4)

> "The heart of man plans his way, but the LORD establishes his steps." (16:9)

> "The king's heart is a stream of water in the hand of the LORD; he turns it wherever he will." (21:1)

- God's creation of the wicked **for** judgment is stated as straightforward fact
- The heart genuinely plans, yet God establishes the outcome
- The king's heart is genuinely his, yet directed by God like water in His hand
- Both agency and divine direction are simultaneously real
- No libertarian freedom required or mentioned
- The wicked are genuinely wicked; God's purpose in creating them doesn't mitigate their guilt

#### Romans 9:17-23: God's Sovereign Purposes

> "For the Scripture says to Pharaoh, 'For this very purpose I have raised you up, that I might show my power in you, and that my name might be proclaimed in all the earth.' So then he has mercy on whomever he wills, and he hardens whomever he wills. You will say to me then, 'Why does he still find fault? For who can resist his will?' But who are you, O man, to answer back to God? Will what is molded say to its molder, 'Why have you made me like this?' Has the potter no right over the clay, to make out of the same lump one vessel for honorable use and another for dishonorable use?" (vv. 17-21)

- Divine hardening is presented as an exercise of sovereign prerogative
- Verse 19's objection ("Why does he still find fault?") is **the exact objection Molinists raise**
- Paul's response is not "You misunderstand. God doesn't actually determine this; He just has middle knowledge"
- His response is "Who are you, O man, to answer back to God?" This asserts divine sovereignty over the objection itself
- The potter has rights over the clay to make vessels for dishonorable use
- No appeal to libertarian freedom to resolve the tension
- Paul grounds the answer in divine prerogative, not in a philosophical theory that preserves human autonomy

#### Ephesians 1:11: The Comprehensive Statement

> "In him we have obtained an inheritance, having been predestined according to the purpose of him who **works all things according to the counsel of his will**."

This is perhaps the most comprehensive single statement of divine sovereignty in Scripture. Note its features:

- **"All things"**: Not "most things," not "as many things as libertarian freedom permits," not "all things except human choices." **All things** without qualification or exception
- **"Works"** (ἐνεργέω): Active causation, not passive permission; God is the one energizing and effectuating all things
- **"According to the counsel of his will"**: The ground is internal to God (His counsel, His will), not external constraints (what creatures would freely do)
- The context is predestination (v. 11a) and inheritance (salvation), meaning the "all things" explicitly includes soteriological choices
- No hint that human choices form an exception to "all things"
- No libertarian machinery invoked to preserve human agency

**If any verse should create the "author of evil" problem, it's this one.** God works **all things**, which must include evil things, according to His will. Yet Paul presents this as cause for praise ("to the praise of his glory," v. 12), not as a theological problem requiring resolution. The comprehensive scope of divine sovereignty is stated as the very foundation of Christian confidence and worship.

#### Philippians 2:12-13: Divine Working and Human Working

> "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure."

- God works **the willing itself**, not just the circumstances around the willing
- Human working and divine working are simultaneous, not sequential
- No ability to "do otherwise" is suggested or required for the command to make sense
- Responsibility exists precisely because the willing is genuinely yours, though God works it
- Paul sees no contradiction between "work out your own" and "God works in you to will"

#### Luke 22:22: Determination and Culpability

> "For the Son of Man goes as it has been determined, but woe to that man by whom he is betrayed!"

- Divine determination: explicit and absolute
- Human culpability: total ("woe to that man")
- Explanation of how both are true: completely absent
- Scripture asserts both without requiring libertarian machinery to reconcile them
- Judas's betrayal flows from his own character (John 12:6, he was a thief), yet it was determined

#### Acts 13:48: Appointment and Belief

> "And when the Gentiles heard this, they began rejoicing and glorifying the word of the Lord, and as many as were appointed to eternal life believed."

- Divine appointment is logically prior to believing
- Yet the believing is genuine. They heard, rejoiced, glorified, and believed
- No suggestion that divine appointment negates the reality of their faith
- The text presents appointment → belief as natural sequence, not as problematic
- Their belief is truly theirs (they are the ones who believed), yet it occurs because they were appointed

#### John 6:37, 44, 65: Sovereign Grace in Salvation

> "All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out." (v. 37)

> "No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him. And I will raise him up on the last day." (v. 44)

> "This is why I told you that no one can come to me unless it is granted him by the Father." (v. 65)

- Coming to Christ is certain for all the Father gives (v. 37)
- Coming to Christ is impossible without Father's drawing (v. 44) or granting (v. 65)
- The drawing/granting is effectual. All who are drawn come
- Yet the coming is genuinely theirs: "whoever comes to me"
- No libertarian freedom is invoked; instead, inability without divine enabling is emphasized
- Divine gift and human coming are both real and simultaneous

#### Isaiah 10:5-15: Assyria as God's Instrument

> "Woe to Assyria, the rod of my anger; the staff in their hands is my fury! Against a godless nation I send him... But he does not so intend, and his heart does not so think; but it is in his heart to destroy... When the Lord has finished all his work on Mount Zion and on Jerusalem, he will punish the speech of the arrogant heart of the king of Assyria and the boastful look in his eyes." (vv. 5-7, 12)

- God sends Assyria as His instrument of judgment
- Assyria's intent is evil (to destroy for its own sake)
- God will punish Assyria for the evil intent
- Same action, different intents, different moral evaluations
- God's use of Assyria doesn't mitigate Assyria's guilt
- Assyria's evil intent is genuinely its own, yet God sends it

#### The Consistent Pattern Confirmed

Across all these texts (spanning narrative, law, wisdom literature, prophets, gospels, epistles, and Acts), the pattern is **invariant**:

1. **Divine sovereignty is exhaustive**: God determines, predestines, hardens, sends delusion, appoints to salvation, works **all things**, draws effectually, establishes steps, directs hearts
2. **Human responsibility is absolute**: No mitigation, no excuse, full culpability assigned to those who act according to their evil desires
3. **No libertarian freedom invoked**: Scripture never says "they could have done otherwise in identical circumstances" as the ground of responsibility
4. **Responsibility grounded in biblical categories**: Intent (what they meant), character (evil heart, pleasure in unrighteousness), willing action (they chose according to their nature)
5. **No explanation of mechanism**: Scripture asserts both divine decree and human responsibility without philosophical resolution and rebukes objections to this (Romans 9:20)

**This is the biblical data across every genre and every major biblical author.** Any system that requires libertarian freedom to preserve responsibility has imported a category Scripture never uses and never requires.

### B. Counterfactuals Don't Require Libertarian Freedom

The Molinist proof-texts for middle knowledge don't establish what they claim. Counterfactuals can be true in a Compatibilist framework without requiring libertarian indeterminacy.

#### 1 Samuel 23:10-13: David's Inquiry

> "Then David said, 'O LORD, the God of Israel, your servant has surely heard that Saul seeks to come to Keilah, to destroy the city on my account. Will the men of Keilah surrender me into his hand? Will Saul come down, as your servant has heard? O LORD, the God of Israel, please tell your servant.' And the LORD said, 'He will come down.' Then David said, 'Will the men of Keilah surrender me and my men into the hand of Saul?' And the LORD said, 'They will surrender you.'"

**Molinist interpretation**: God knows what free creatures would do in circumstances that don't actualize. This requires middle knowledge grounded in libertarian freedom.

**Compatibilist account**: God knows what would happen because He knows:
- The character and desires He has given to Saul and the men of Keilah
- What He would decree in those circumstances if they were to obtain
- How human nature (which He created) functions under various conditions
- The dispositions, motivations, and contextual factors that would lead to certain choices

The counterfactual is grounded in:
- God's comprehensive knowledge of all possibilities
- God's intimate knowledge of the natures He has created (Psalm 139:1-6)
- God's knowledge of what He Himself would decree
- Not in libertarian indeterminacy external to His decree

#### Matthew 11:21-23: Judgment on Unrepentant Cities

> "Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes."

**Molinist interpretation**: Jesus knows what would have happened under different circumstances. Tyre and Sidon would have freely repented. This requires middle knowledge.

**Compatibilist account**: 
- Under Compatibilism, repentance flows from character transformation (Ezekiel 36:26, heart of stone → heart of flesh)
- God knows what would have happened because He knows:
  - What internal conditions (desires, beliefs, heart states) would result from those mighty works in Tyre and Sidon
  - How He created human nature to respond to various kinds of evidence and experience
  - What He would decree regarding heart-softening in those circumstances
- The counterfactual is grounded in God's knowledge of how the mighty works would affect character, producing the desire to repent
- "Would have repented" doesn't require libertarian freedom. It requires knowing the causal relationship between divine works and human responses

#### 1 Corinthians 2:8: The Rulers' Ignorance

> "None of the rulers of this age understood this, for if they had understood, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory."

**Molinist interpretation**: Paul states a counterfactual, what would not have occurred given different knowledge. This requires middle knowledge of what they would freely do.

**Compatibilist account**:
- This is compatible with God having decreed both their ignorance and the crucifixion
- The counterfactual simply reflects that crucifixion was incompatible with genuine understanding of Christ's glory
- God knows that had He given them understanding (which He could have done), they would not have crucified Christ (because understanding His glory would produce appropriate reverence)
- The counterfactual is grounded in God's knowledge of the incompatibility between true understanding and hostile action, not in libertarian indeterminacy

### The Grounding Objection Fails

Craig argues that without libertarian freedom, counterfactuals lose their ground. In a deterministic framework, "what would happen" allegedly collapses into "what will happen," eliminating any distinct truth value for subjunctive conditionals.

**The response**: This assumes what it needs to prove, that counterfactuals require libertarian indeterminacy to be true. But counterfactuals are true in Compatibilism through:

1. **God's knowledge of His own decree**: God knows what He would decree in any circumstance
2. **God's knowledge of created natures**: God knows how creatures with particular characters and desires would respond to various circumstances
3. **God's knowledge of all possibilities**: God comprehensively knows all possible worlds and what would occur in each based on His potential decrees

The truth-maker for "If X were the case, Y would occur" is not libertarian indeterminacy but:
- God's knowledge of how He structured causation
- God's knowledge of creaturely natures (which He created)
- God's knowledge of what He would decree if X were actual

Counterfactuals are grounded in **divine omniscience and sovereign decree**, not in libertarian indeterminacy. The Molinist reads their metaphysics into these texts, but the texts themselves never require it.

## 5. The Logical Incoherence of the Molinist Charge

Having shown that Scripture never requires libertarian freedom and that Molinism imposes extrabiblical categories on the text, we now turn to internal critique: **Even on its own terms, Molinism's "author of evil" charge against Compatibilism is logically incoherent.**

### A. The "Author of Evil" Charge Requires a Standard Molinism Cannot Provide

Craig's argument assumes there's a meaningful moral difference between:

1. **Compatibilism**: God determines circumstances and internal states such that a person will certainly choose evil (though the choice flows from their own desires and character)
2. **Molinism**: God knows what a person would freely choose in given circumstances and actualizes those circumstances, knowing with certainty that evil will result

**But what is the morally relevant difference?**

In both systems:
- God has comprehensive foreknowledge that evil E will occur if He takes action A (decreeing/actualizing)
- God could prevent E by not taking action A
- God takes action A anyway, knowing E will result with certainty
- E occurs as a direct consequence of God's decision
- God's decision is the ultimate reason E occurs in the actual world rather than not occurring

**The Molinist response**: "But in our system, the person could have done otherwise, so God isn't causing the evil. He's just actualizing circumstances where He knows what they'll freely choose."

**The problem**: This response is logically incoherent because the "ability to do otherwise" cuts both ways:

- If the person's ability to "do otherwise" exonerates God from responsibility (even though God's actualization is causally necessary for E), then by parallel reasoning, God's ability to "actualize otherwise" should exonerate the person (even though their choice is causally necessary for E)
- Both God and the human have alternative possibilities available
- Both make choices that result in E
- Both could have chosen differently
- If "could have done otherwise" removes moral responsibility, it removes it from both agents or from neither

**Molinists cannot explain why "could have done otherwise" removes divine responsibility but not human responsibility.** The principle is applied asymmetrically without justification. The charge is incoherent on its own terms.

### B. The Sourcehood Objection Backfires

Craig argues that moral responsibility requires being the "ultimate source" of one's actions. Since God is the ultimate source in Compatibilism (determining both circumstances and internal states), humans cannot be genuinely responsible. They're just instruments.

**The problems**:

#### 1. This Proves Too Much

In Molinism, God is still the ultimate source of:
- Your existence (you wouldn't exist without God's creative act)
- Your nature, including your character dispositions, cognitive faculties, and volitional capacities
- The circumstances you're placed in
- The options available to you
- The evidential environment that shapes your beliefs
- The point in history where you exist with all its accumulated influences

If being the ultimate source of **any** contributing factor removes responsibility from downstream agents, then Molinism still makes God the ultimate source, just with extra steps. The human's "free" choice is still the product of factors God ultimately sourced.

#### 2. Scripture Never Teaches "Ultimate Sourcehood" as the Criterion

Biblical responsibility is consistently grounded in:

- **Intent/desire**: Matthew 5:28, "everyone who looks at a woman with lustful intent has already committed adultery with her in his heart" (the thought, not just the action, creates guilt)
- **Character**: Matthew 7:17-18, "every healthy tree bears good fruit, but the diseased tree bears bad fruit... a healthy tree cannot bear bad fruit, nor can a diseased tree bear good fruit" (actions flow from nature)
- **Knowledge**: Luke 12:47-48, "that servant who knew his master's will but did not get ready or act according to his will, will receive a severe beating. But the one who did not know, and did what deserved a beating, will receive a light beating" (different judgment based on knowledge)
- **Willing action**: Romans 7:15-20, "For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate... So now it is no longer I who do it, but sin that dwells within me... For I have the desire to do what is right, but not the ability to carry it out" (Paul distinguishes willing sin from unwilling sin, but he's responsible for both because both flow from his state)

Nowhere does Scripture say: "You're only responsible if you're the ultimate source independent of all prior causation." That's a philosophical import, not a biblical category.

#### 3. The Manipulation Intuition Is Question-Begging

Craig's "puppet" analogy assumes that determination equals manipulation equals coercion. But this conflates categorically different things:

**Manipulation/coercion in Scripture** refers to:
- External force against one's will (Genesis 39:7-12, Potiphar's wife pressuring Joseph, which he resists)
- Threats that create fear overriding desire (Daniel 3, bow or die)
- Deception that causes false beliefs leading to unwanted actions (Genesis 3, the serpent deceives Eve)

**Compatibilist determination** means:
- Your choices flow from your nature, desires, and character
- They are authentically yours, not alien impositions
- You act according to what you most want (given your character state)
- No external force overrides your will

**The puppet comparison fails**:
- The puppet has no desires of its own; you do
- The puppet's movements don't express its character; yours do
- The puppet is moved against its nature; you act according to yours
- Scripture holds people responsible precisely when their evil flows from their own evil hearts (Jeremiah 17:9, "The heart is deceitful above all things"; Mark 7:21-23, "from within, out of the heart of man, come evil thoughts, sexual immorality, theft, murder, adultery, coveting, wickedness, deceit, sensuality, envy, slander, pride, foolishness. All these evil things come from within")

The manipulation intuition only works if you assume libertarian freedom is what makes something "truly yours." But that's the very thing under dispute. The argument is circular.

### C. Molinism's "Actualization" Problem: The Author Analogy

Craig objects that Compatibilism makes God actively bring about evil, not merely permit it. But Molinism faces the identical problem.

**Under Molinism:**
- God knows with certainty what will happen if He actualizes world W
- Evil E will certainly occur in W
- God actualizes W anyway
- Therefore, God's action (actualizing W) is the but-for cause of E occurring

This is **not** mere permission. Permission means allowing something you could prevent without your positive action being required for it to occur. But in Molinism:
- E won't occur unless God actualizes W
- God actualizes W knowing E will occur with certainty
- Therefore, God's act of world-actualization is causally necessary for E

**The Molinist response**: "But God doesn't determine the evil choice; He only actualizes the circumstances where He knows what will freely happen."

**The reply**: This is a distinction without moral difference if the "author of evil" charge is what matters. Consider the author analogy (noting that all Creator-creature analogies are analogical, not univocal; this illustrates causal structure, not ontological parity):

An author writes a tragedy in which a character commits betrayal. Every element of the betrayal (the character's motives, the circumstances, the opportunity, the very existence of the betrayer) exists **only** because the author wills the story into being. The author is the ultimate cause of everything in the narrative, including the evil. Yet the author is not morally complicit in the character's treachery because:

1. **The character's evil intent belongs to the story's moral world, not the author's nature**. The author decrees that evil be depicted, but does not share the evil motive. The author may detest the evil while ordaining that it occur in the narrative for plot purposes.

2. **The author's purpose in including the betrayal may be entirely redemptive**. To reveal character, advance the plot toward resolution, demonstrate justice, create the conditions for heroic virtue to shine, or bring about a greater good.

3. **The betrayal is genuinely the character's**. It flows from the character's nature, desires, and choices as written. The author doesn't "reach into" the story and manually puppet the betrayer. The character's evil deed is organic to who the character is.

Now apply this to divine decree:

**In Compatibilism:**
- God decrees that evil E occur for wise purposes (ultimate causation)
- The evil intent belongs to the creature's moral world, not God's nature. God ordains that evil occur but does not share the evil desire
- God's purpose in ordaining E is redemptive/just (Genesis 50:20, saving many lives; Acts 2:23, accomplishing redemption)
- The evil genuinely flows from the creature's own desires and character (James 1:14-15, lured by his own desire)

**In Molinism:**
- God actualizes world W knowing E will occur (ultimate causation)
- The evil intent belongs to the creature's moral world, not God's nature
- God's purpose in actualizing W is presumably wise/redemptive
- The evil flows from the creature's "free" choice

**The structures are identical regarding ultimate causation.** In both systems:
- Nothing happens without God's decision to make it actual (decree or actualization)
- God has comprehensive foreknowledge of all outcomes
- God could prevent E but doesn't
- God's decision is causally necessary for E to occur in the actual world

The only difference is Molinism adds the claim "but the creature could have done otherwise in identical circumstances." But as established in Section V.A, this doesn't remove divine causation. It just adds an extra layer. If "could have done otherwise" exonerates God from being evil's author, it should equally exonerate the human (after all, God could have actualized otherwise). The principle cuts both ways, making the appeal incoherent.

**The real question isn't "Who is the ultimate cause?" (In both systems, God is.) The question is "Does ultimate causation make one the author of evil in the morally culpable sense?"**

The answer depends not on causal ultimacy but on:
- **Intent**: Is the ultimate cause's purpose evil or redemptive?
- **Nature**: Does the ultimate cause delight in evil for its own sake?
- **Mediation**: Does evil flow from the moral agent's own character and desires?

Molinism cannot explain why their system escapes the "author of evil" charge when it preserves the same ultimate causal structure as Compatibilism. The author analogy reveals that **ordination of evil for wise purposes ≠ moral authorship of evil.** That distinction exists in both Compatibilism and Molinism, or in neither. Craig's critique, if valid, destroys both systems equally.

### D. Middle Knowledge Creates Worse Problems Than It Solves

If God cannot make someone freely choose what they would not freely choose (Craig's claim), then Molinism generates theological catastrophes:

#### 1. God's Saving Will Can Be Finally Thwarted

If someone would freely reject Christ in all feasible worlds where they exist, God cannot save them. The person's libertarian choice places an absolute limit on divine saving power. This directly contradicts:

- **Ephesians 1:11**: "In him we have obtained an inheritance, having been predestined according to the purpose of him who works all things according to the counsel of his will." Not "most things" or "as many things as libertarian freedom permits," but **all things**
- **Isaiah 46:10**: "My counsel shall stand, and I will accomplish all my purpose." God's purposes are not subject to creaturely veto
- **Daniel 4:35**: "He does according to his will among the host of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth; and none can stay his hand or say to him, 'What have you done?'" **None** can stay His hand, not even by libertarian free refusal
- **Job 42:2**: "I know that you can do all things, and that no purpose of yours can be thwarted." Not "almost all things" or "all things consistent with libertarian freedom"

In Molinism, if God's saving purpose for an individual is that they be saved, but they would freely reject Him in all feasible worlds, God's purpose **is thwarted**. The creature's will is final. That is not the God of Scripture.

#### 2. God's Knowledge Becomes Dependent on Creatures

Middle knowledge is supposed to be logically prior to God's decree. This means God's knowledge of what creatures would do is not based on what He decrees. But then what grounds it?

- It cannot be grounded in the creatures themselves (they don't exist yet at this logical moment)
- It cannot be grounded in God's will (middle knowledge is prior to the decree of God's will in the logical order)
- It cannot be grounded in circumstances (those don't exist yet either)

Craig responds that counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are "just true," brute facts that God knows but doesn't determine. But this creates **logical dualism**: if counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are true independent of God's decree, they constitute **necessary truths external to God**, a second eternal principle beside Him. This means:

- There exists a truth-set (all counterfactuals of creaturely freedom) that is:
  - Not created by God
  - Not decreed by God
  - Not dependent on God's will
  - Eternally existent alongside God
- God's knowledge is informed by this external truth-set
- God's creative options are constrained by this truth-set

**This is metaphysical dualism**: two self-existent realities (God and the brute fact truth-set of what free creatures would do). This makes:
- God's knowledge dependent on facts outside Himself
- God's knowledge dependent on something He didn't create or decree
- God's knowledge dependent on the natures of possible creatures who don't yet exist

**This violates divine aseity**, God's complete self-sufficiency and independence. Scripture teaches:

- **Acts 17:25**: God "is not served by human hands, as though he needed anything, since he himself gives to all mankind life and breath and everything"
- **Psalm 50:12**: "If I were hungry, I would not tell you, for the world and its fullness are mine"
- **Romans 11:34-36**: "For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor? Or who has given a gift to him that he might be repaid? For from him and through him and to him are all things"

A God whose knowledge is informed by brute facts external to His decree is dependent on those facts. That's not aseity. That's contingency.

#### 3. It Assumes What It Needs to Prove

The entire system is an elaborate philosophical construct built to preserve libertarian freedom. But Scripture never teaches that libertarian freedom is necessary for moral responsibility. Therefore:

- Molinism invents a complex metaphysical apparatus (three moments of knowledge, feasible worlds, middle knowledge grounded in counterfactuals)
- To solve a problem that exists only if you assume libertarian freedom is required
- But that assumption is never proven from Scripture
- Therefore, the system is an extrabiblical philosophical solution to an extrabiblical philosophical problem

This is philosophy masquerading as exegesis.

### E. Philosophical Reinforcement: Compatibilist Analytic Philosophy Confirms the Critique

The preceding critiques demonstrate Molinism's incoherence from an exegetical and theological perspective. But our case receives substantial reinforcement from analytic philosophy itself. Steven B. Cowan's review of Kenneth Keathley's *Salvation and Sovereignty: A Molinist Approach* (*Themelios* 35.3) provides rigorous philosophical critique from a compatibilist perspective, confirming several of our key arguments and exposing additional weaknesses we must address. Cowan is himself a compatibilist philosopher whose published work includes papers arguing against middle knowledge, making his analysis particularly valuable as confirmation from within analytic philosophy.

#### Points of Overlap: Where Our Critique and Cowan's Analysis Align

**1. Counterfactuals Don't Require Libertarian Freedom (Section IV.B)**

Cowan explicitly affirms our argument: "A compatibilist can easily grant the truth of these conditionals but insist that this is best explained by these conditionals not being counterfactuals of freedom. Rather, God can know and utilize these counterfactuals in ordering his providence precisely because the agents have only compatibilist freedom."

This is precisely our point in Section IV.B. The truth-maker for counterfactuals is not libertarian indeterminacy but God's comprehensive knowledge of His own decrees, created natures, and causal structures. Molinists beg the question by assuming counterfactuals require middle knowledge grounded in libertarian freedom.

**2. The Contingency Ambiguity (Section III.A, Burden of Proof)**

Cowan exposes Keathley's exploitation of ambiguity in the term "could": "The term 'could' here is notoriously ambiguous. And Keathley appears to exploit this ambiguity to argue for creaturely libertarian freedom in Scripture... Keathley insists, 'God's statements about destroying Israel and starting over with Moses were contingently true and describe real possibilities' (p. 33). Yes, but what kind of possibilities? I would argue that they are simply logical possibilities."

This confirms our Section III argument: Molinists assume "could have been otherwise" means libertarian freedom when it can simply mean logical possibility. The question-begging is flagrant. As Cowan notes, "Keathley appears to think that true contingency and conditionality rule out necessity and determination, but this is not the case."

**3. Election Remains Conditional Despite Molinist Claims (Section V.D.1)**

Cowan dismantles Keathley's claim that Molinism provides unconditional election: "It is not plausible to call this model a version of unconditional election... Why is person S elect? Because God (via middle knowledge) saw that he would choose to have faith in the possible world that God made actual... My election is conditioned on my choice. Moreover, whether or not I am electable at all depends on what I would do in the various settings God could place me in."

This supports our critique that Molinism makes God's purposes defeasible, subject to creaturely choice. If election depends on what the creature would freely do, it is by definition conditional, regardless of semantic games about "God chooses the world."

#### Additional Critical Points from Analytic Philosophy

Beyond confirming our arguments, Cowan raises several significant critiques we did not address, which we must now incorporate:

**4. The Meticulous Providence Problem: Scheme A vs. Scheme B**

Cowan presents a dilemma fatal to Molinist claims about divine sovereignty: "If God has a plan for how he wants the world to go that is logically antecedent to his cognizance of the truth-values of counterfactuals, then he would be astronomically lucky if there turned out to be a feasible world that matched his plan (because what worlds are feasible is determined by creaturely choices, not God). I call this version of Molinism Scheme A Molinism."

The alternative is equally problematic: "For Molinism to be credible, the Molinist has to adopt Scheme B Molinism and believe that God first takes note of what counterfactuals are true, and thus which worlds are feasible, and then comes up with a plan for how he would like things to go in the actual world, given his severely delimited options. But... this hardly deserves to be called a meticulous view of providence. On Scheme B Molinism, the extent of God's providential control in history may be likened to a man choosing which movie to play on his DVD player where all the playable movies are written, directed, and acted by others."

**This creates a dilemma with no escape.** Either:
- **(Scheme A)** God has a plan independent of middle knowledge, making it astronomically lucky if a feasible world matches (undermining the entire Molinist mechanism)
- **(Scheme B)** God adapts His plan to fit feasible worlds, making Him a passive selector among options created by creaturely counterfactuals (hardly sovereignty)

Both options collapse Molinism's claim to preserve meticulous providence. This confirms our Section V.D.2 point about divine dependence on external facts.

**5. Libertarian Freedom as "Choosing for No Reason"**

Cowan identifies the absurdity at libertarianism's core: "It does mean that agents still choose (say) doing x over doing not-x for absolutely no reason! When answering the question why Smith chose to play ball rather than stay home, ultimately the libertarian (in order to avoid determinism) has to say that that is just what Smith chose to do. If pressed as to why he so chose, the answer has to be: for no reason at all! I contend that such freedom, if we have it, undermines rather than supports our moral responsibility."

This confirms that libertarian categories, far from grounding responsibility, actually destroy it. If my choice is ultimately uncaused by reasons, character, or desires, it's random, not responsible. Scripture grounds responsibility in intent and character (Section VI.B), never in causeless libertarian choice.

**6. The Ambulatory Model Fails to Preserve Monergism**

Keathley attempts to preserve monergism through an "Ambulatory Model" where grace operates automatically unless resisted. Cowan exposes the fatal flaw: "It is obvious that refraining in a case like this requires an active choice! 'Refraining' here means something like 'acquiescing,' and acquiescing is doing! My gaining saving faith, on the Ambulatory Model, clearly requires my cooperation, and cooperation on the part of the sinner is the antithesis of monergism."

Passive non-resistance is still volitional action. The model cannot escape synergism. This supports our broader critique that Molinism cannot maintain its soteriological commitments because libertarian freedom necessarily makes salvation depend on creaturely cooperation.

**7. The "Two Wills" Objection Fails**

Cowan defends the Calvinist "two wills" doctrine against Keathley's incoherence charge: "Why may we not say (roughly following the logic of John Piper) that God has a prima facie (all things being equal) desire to save all, but an ultima facie (all things considered) desire to accomplish some other good ends that overrides his prima facie desire to save all and thus necessitates saving only some? There is nothing at all incoherent about this distinction."

He provides an analogy: A physician desires to save people in a foreign land but remains home to save people in his homeland. "All things being equal, he desires to work to save both groups of people. But all things are not equal... it doesn't follow that the doctor doesn't love the folks in the foreign land."

This defends against the Molinist charge that Compatibilism makes God insincere in His offers. The distinction between prima facie and ultima facie desires is coherent and preserves both divine sincerity and sovereignty. While we don't rely on this defense in our paper, Cowan's response shows the "two wills" objection fails on philosophical grounds.

**8. God's Freedom Doesn't Require Libertarian Freedom**

Against the claim that denying God's libertarian freedom means He "had to create this particular world" and "this is the only possible world," Cowan responds: "I grant that God, given his plans and purposes for the world, could not have created any other world than this one. But it does not follow that there is no possible world in which God's plans and purposes are different... I see no non-question-begging reason why orthodox Christianity has to be committed to understanding God's freedom in libertarian terms. On a compatibilist view of freedom, God is free in creating if the world he creates results from his own desires and intentions."

This dismantles the argument that Compatibilism applied to God collapses into necessitarianism. God's freedom is grounded in acting according to His nature and desires, not in having libertarian alternatives. The same applies to humans.

**9. The Grounding Objection Stands Unresolved**

Cowan clarifies what the grounding objection actually is (versus Keathley's misrepresentation): "The grounding objection has nothing to do with whether or not God can create libertarianly free creatures. It's about whether or not counterfactuals have truth-value, and thus about whether or not God can know them. There are reasons to think that counterfactuals lack truth-value, which means that middle knowledge is incoherent."

This is more fundamental than our presentation. The issue isn't merely that counterfactuals don't require libertarian freedom (our Section IV.B), but that **libertarian counterfactuals may have no truth-value at all**. If they're not grounded in divine decree, created natures, or circumstances (since these don't exist at the logical moment of middle knowledge), they're grounded in nothing. Brute facts with no ontological foundation cannot be known because there's nothing there to know.

#### Closing the Gaps: What Cowan's Analysis Didn't Address

Cowan's review is philosophically rigorous but leaves theological and biblical gaps we must close:

**Gap 1: Divine Simplicity**

Cowan doesn't address the divine simplicity problem we raised in Section VI.C. Middle knowledge requires:
- Logical moments separating God's knowledge from His will
- God's knowledge being informed by facts external to His decree
- Composition in God (knowledge prior to will)

This violates divine simplicity and aseity. Our Section VI.C argument stands independently and must be maintained.

**Gap 2: Scriptural Authority**

Cowan engages Molinism philosophically but doesn't mount the comprehensive exegetical case we present in Section IV. He notes biblical problems but doesn't systematically demonstrate the pattern across narrative, law, wisdom, prophets, gospels, and epistles showing that:
- Divine sovereignty is exhaustive in every text
- Human responsibility never requires libertarian freedom
- Responsibility is grounded in biblical categories (intent, character, willing action)

Our exegetical work (Section IV) is non-negotiable and must be preserved.

**Gap 3: The Idolatry Charge**

Cowan critiques Molinism's logical problems but doesn't make the theological judgment we render in Section VII: **The god of Molinism is not YHWH but an idol.** This is not mere philosophical disagreement; it's theological apostasy. A god whose:
- Purposes can be thwarted
- Knowledge depends on external facts
- Saving will is defeasible
- Sovereignty is constrained by libertarian categories

This is a different deity. Cowan treats Molinism as a flawed philosophical system. We recognize it as constructing a false god. This distinction is critical and must be maintained.

**Gap 4: The "Author of Evil" Charge Reversal**

Cowan doesn't explicitly reverse the "author of evil" charge against Molinism. We do (Section V.C). Both systems involve God's decision being causally necessary for evil to occur in the actual world. If ultimate causation makes God the author of evil, both systems fail. But ultimate causation doesn't determine moral authorship; intent, nature, and mediation do. This turns the charge back on Molinism: they charge Compatibilism with making God evil's author while constructing a system with identical causal structure.

#### Synthesis: The Strength of Combined Critique

Our argument is now reinforced at every level:

**Burden of Proof (Section III)**: Confirmed by Cowan's demonstration that Molinists exploit ambiguity and beg the question by assuming contingency equals libertarian freedom.

**Counterfactuals (Section IV.B)**: Validated by Cowan's affirmation that compatibilists can grant counterfactuals without libertarian indeterminacy, and his deeper point that libertarian counterfactuals may lack truth-value entirely.

**Logical Incoherence (Section V)**: Strengthened by Cowan's Scheme A/B dilemma (meticulous providence fails on either horn), the "choosing for no reason" absurdity, and the Ambulatory Model's collapse into synergism.

**Divine Attributes (Section VI.C)**: Our divine simplicity argument stands unchallenged. Cowan didn't address it, and Molinism has no answer.

**Idolatry (Section VII)**: Our theological verdict remains. Rigorous philosophical critique from analytic philosophy confirms Molinism is logically incoherent and scripturally unfounded. It constructs a false god.

The conclusion strengthens: **The case against Molinism is complete from both exegetical-theological and philosophical-analytical perspectives.** When compatibilist analytic philosophy identifies fatal problems (defeasible election, choosing-for-no-reason absurdity, meticulous providence failure, grounding objection unresolved), and the best defenses require either denying libertarian freedom or accepting that God's sovereignty is reduced to "choosing which movie to play," the system has collapsed under its own weight.

Molinism is not a minor error in theological method. It is philosophy masquerading as exegesis, importing libertarian categories to construct an idol whose attributes contradict Scripture's revelation of YHWH's exhaustive sovereignty, simplicity, and indefeasible purposes.

## 6. The Positive Case: Why Compatibilism Doesn't Make God the Author of Evil

Having demolished Molinism's critique, we now present the positive case: **Compatibilism preserves both divine sovereignty and human responsibility without making God the author of evil because Scripture itself makes distinctions the Molinist ignores.**

### A. Biblical Distinctions Molinists Ignore

Scripture operates with categories that resolve the alleged tension without requiring libertarian freedom:

#### 1. Being the Ultimate Ordainer vs. Being the Moral Agent

Scripture distinguishes between:
- **God as ultimate ordainer**: He decrees that evil occur for wise purposes
- **The creature as moral agent**: The creature desires and commits the evil

Both are true simultaneously without contradiction.

**Genesis 50:20**: "As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good"
- Same events
- Joseph's brothers: evil intent (meant evil)
- God: good intent (meant it for good)
- Both "meant" it; both had purposes regarding the same events
- Moral evaluation based on intent, not on ultimate causation

**Acts 2:23**: "Delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God, you crucified"
- God's role: definite plan (He ordained it)
- Human role: you crucified (you are the guilty agents)
- Both are simultaneously true
- No tension presented

#### 2. Primary vs. Secondary Causation

- **Primary cause**: God upholds all things (Colossians 1:17), determines all outcomes (Ephesians 1:11), works all things (Romans 8:28)
- **Secondary causes**: Humans genuinely act according to their nature, making real choices that flow from their character

Both causal levels are real; neither negates the other. This is the classical distinction that preserves both divine sovereignty and creaturely agency.

**Proverbs 16:9**: "The heart of man plans his way, but the LORD establishes his steps"
- Human planning is real (secondary cause)
- Divine establishing is ultimate (primary cause)
- Both are affirmed without explanation

**Philippians 2:12-13**: "Work out your own salvation... for it is God who works in you"
- Human working is real and commanded
- Divine working is ultimate and effective (works both the willing and the working)
- No contradiction presented

#### 3. Different Moral Evaluation Based on Intent

Scripture judges the same event differently based on intent, not based on ultimate causation.

**Isaiah 10:5-15**: Assyria as God's rod of anger
- God sends Assyria: "Against a godless nation I send him" (v. 6)
- Assyria's intent: "But he does not so intend, and his heart does not so think; but it is in his heart to destroy" (v. 7)
- God will punish Assyria: "When the Lord has finished all his work... he will punish the speech of the arrogant heart of the king of Assyria" (v. 12)
- Same action
- God's intent: judicial punishment, accomplishing His purposes
- Assyria's intent: proud destruction for its own sake
- God's use of Assyria doesn't mitigate Assyria's guilt because the guilt is based on intent, not ultimate causation

**This is the biblical pattern**: Ultimate causation doesn't determine moral evaluation. Intent does.

### B. The Proper Definition of "Author of Evil"

To be the "author" of evil in the morally culpable sense requires meeting specific biblical criteria. Examine what Scripture actually says:

#### 1. Evil Intent/Desire

Scripture consistently judges based on heart motive:

- **1 Samuel 16:7**: "The LORD sees not as man sees: man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart"
- **Proverbs 21:2**: "Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs the heart"
- **Matthew 5:28**: "Everyone who looks at a woman with lustful intent has already committed adultery with her in his heart"

The heart's intent is what creates moral culpability.

#### 2. Evil Character

Being the kind of being that delights in evil for its own sake:

- **Psalm 5:4**: "For you are not a God who delights in wickedness; evil may not dwell with you"
- **Habakkuk 1:13**: "You who are of purer eyes than to see evil and cannot look at wrong"

God's nature is incompatible with delighting in evil.

#### 3. Direct Agency Where Evil Originates

**James 1:13-15** is the definitive text:

> "Let no one say when he is tempted, 'I am being tempted by God,' for God cannot be tempted with evil, and he himself tempts no one. But each person is tempted when he is lured and enticed **by his own desire**. Then desire when it has conceived gives birth to sin, and sin when it is fully grown brings forth death."

Notice what James doesn't say: "God doesn't tempt anyone because people have libertarian free will." He provides two grounds for God not being the tempter:

1. **God's nature is incompatible with evil**: "God cannot be tempted with evil" (His nature makes Him impervious to evil's pull)
2. **Evil desire originates from within the person**: "by his own desire" (the internal source)

The internal origin of evil desire is what absolves God and indicts the human. Not libertarian freedom. Not the ability to do otherwise. The source of the desire.

**Supporting texts**:

- **Mark 7:21-23**: "For from within, out of the heart of man, come evil thoughts, sexual immorality, theft, murder, adultery, coveting, wickedness, deceit, sensuality, envy, slander, pride, foolishness. All these evil things come from within, and they defile a person"
- **Jeremiah 17:9**: "The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately sick; who can understand it?"

Evil originates from the human heart, not from God. That's the biblical category for "author of evil," the one from whom evil desire originates as an expression of their nature.

#### God Meets None of These Criteria

**1. His intent in ordaining evil is always redemptive or just:**
- The cross: ordained for salvation (Acts 2:23, 4:27-28)
- Joseph's suffering: ordained to save many lives (Genesis 50:20)
- Assyria's conquest: ordained as judicial punishment (Isaiah 10:5-7)
- All suffering: works together for good for those who love Him (Romans 8:28)

God ordains that evil occur, but His intent is never evil. It's always to accomplish redemptive or judicial purposes.

**2. His character is perfectly holy:**
- **Habakkuk 1:13**: "You who are of purer eyes than to see evil"
- **James 1:13**: "God cannot be tempted with evil, and he himself tempts no one"
- **1 John 1:5**: "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all"
- **Psalm 5:4**: "You are not a God who delights in wickedness"

God's nature is the polar opposite of evil. He is ontologically incompatible with evil.

**3. Evil flows from the creature's own nature, not from God:**
- **James 1:14-15**: "Each person is tempted when he is lured and enticed **by his own desire**. Then desire when it has conceived gives birth to sin"
- **Mark 7:21-23**: "From within, out of the heart of man, come evil thoughts..."

The evil desire originates in the creature's heart. God's decree doesn't inject evil desire into neutral hearts. It ensures that hearts already inclined toward evil (due to the Fall) act according to their nature. The creature is the direct efficient cause and moral agent; God is the ordaining cause who uses even evil for good purposes without sharing in the evil intent.

### The Compatibilist Position Summarized

God ordains that evil occur (ultimate sovereignty) for wise and good purposes, but the evil itself flows from the creature's own evil nature and desires (genuine agency and responsibility). God's ordination doesn't make the evil His; it makes the execution of His perfect purposes certain, using even the wicked choices of His creatures for redemptive and judicial ends, all without sharing in their evil intent.

**This is not a philosophical innovation.** This is simply affirming what Scripture explicitly teaches while refusing to add the extrabiblical requirement that responsibility needs libertarian freedom.

### C. The Divine Simplicity Connection

Molinists often reject divine simplicity, the doctrine that God's attributes are identical to His essence, that there is no composition or division in God. This rejection is not incidental; it's structurally necessary for Molinism to work.

#### Why Molinism Requires Rejecting Divine Simplicity

**Divine simplicity** entails:
- God's knowledge is identical with His being
- God's will is identical with His being
- **Therefore, what God knows and what God wills cannot be separated or sequenced**. There is no logical moment where God's knowledge exists independent of His will, or vice versa
- All of God's attributes are one undivided reality that is God Himself

**Molinism requires**:
- Middle knowledge **logically prior** to God's decree
- God's knowledge of what creatures would do existing **independent** of what God wills them to do
- A **division** between God's knowledge and God's will
- God's knowledge being **informed by facts external** to His decree (what creatures would freely do)

This creates composition in God, a distinction between His knowledge and His will, with the former depending on facts not grounded in the latter. **This directly violates divine simplicity.**

#### But Divine Simplicity Is Biblical, Not Hellenistic

Molinists often dismiss divine simplicity as "Hellenistic philosophy" imported into Christian theology. This is false. Divine simplicity flows necessarily from Scripture's revelation of God's nature:

**Exodus 3:14**: "God said to Moses, 'I AM WHO I AM'"
- God's essence is pure self-existence (*ipsum esse subsistens*)
- He is not a being among beings, but Being itself
- Pure being admits no composition, no division, no dependence
- He doesn't have existence; He is existence

**Malachi 3:6**: "For I the LORD do not change"
- Immutability requires simplicity
- A composite being can change in its parts or in the relations between its parts
- If God had parts (knowledge separate from will), those parts could vary in relation
- Complete immutability requires complete simplicity

**Acts 17:25**: "Nor is he served by human hands, as though he needed anything, since he himself gives to all mankind life and breath and everything"
- Perfect aseity (self-sufficiency)
- God depends on nothing outside Himself
- If God's knowledge depended on counterfactuals external to His decree, He would depend on something He didn't create
- Aseity requires simplicity, no dependence on anything external

**James 1:17**: "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow due to change"
- No variation means no succession of states
- No shadow means no potentiality (shadows require parts blocking light)
- This is simplicity: no succession, no composition, no potentiality

**Romans 11:34-36**: "For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor? Or who has given a gift to him that he might be repaid? For from him and through him and to him are all things. To him be glory forever. Amen."
- God is not informed by anything outside Himself
- He is the source of all things, not dependent on any
- If middle knowledge required counterfactuals external to God's decree to inform Him, someone would have "counseled" Him or "given a gift" to Him (information)
- This contradicts Paul's rhetorical questions

#### The Theological Collapse

If middle knowledge is logically prior to God's decree, then there is a "logical moment" (even if not temporal) in which:
- God **knows** what creatures would do
- But has not yet **willed** what will actually occur
- His knowledge depends on counterfactual truths external to His will

**This creates a division in God between knowing and willing.** But in classical theism grounded in Scripture's portrayal of divine aseity and simplicity:
- God's knowledge **just is** His being
- God's will **just is** His being
- There is no logical priority, no dependence, no composition
- What God knows flows from what He is; what He wills flows from what He is; and what He is cannot be analyzed into parts or logical moments

**The knockout**: A God whose knowledge can be informed by facts independent of His will, whose purposes can be constrained by creaturely counterfactuals, whose attributes can be separated into logical moments, this is a composite God. And a composite God is not the simple, indivisible, self-existent YHWH of Scripture.

Molinism doesn't just modify a technical doctrine. It **fundamentally redefines the nature of God** from simple to composite, from independent to dependent, from immutable (no internal succession or division) to having logical moments where His knowledge precedes His will. That's not a minor theological adjustment; **it's a different God.**

### D. Compatibilism and the Mystery We Affirm

A potential objection must be addressed: "Compatibilism doesn't explain **how** divine sovereignty and human responsibility cohere. Therefore it's just bare assertion or voluntarism."

**The response**: This objection misunderstands the nature of theological method and the limits of creature comprehension.

**Compatibilism does not claim to explain the mechanism** of how divine decree and human agency both obtain. We do not possess (and Scripture does not provide) a comprehensive metaphysical account that resolves the apparent tension to human satisfaction. What Compatibilism claims is:

1. **Scripture clearly teaches both exhaustive divine sovereignty and genuine human responsibility** (demonstrated in Section IV)
2. **These are not contradictory but complementary truths** held in tension by divine revelation
3. **The coexistence of both is a mystery, not a contradiction**. A mystery being a truth revealed by God that transcends full human comprehension, while contradiction would be affirming both P and not-P in the same sense

**This is not voluntarism** (asserting God's will without ground), nor is it fideism (believing without reason). It is **epistemological humility**, recognizing that:

- **Finite minds cannot fully comprehend infinite ways** (Isaiah 55:8-9, "For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, declares the LORD")
- **God's judgments are unsearchable** (Romans 11:33, "How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways!")
- **God does not owe us an explanation of His mechanisms** (Job 38-41, four chapters of "Where were you when I...?")

**Mystery ≠ Contradiction**:
- **Mystery**: Both divine decree and human agency are true; we don't fully grasp how they cohere in God's economy
- **Contradiction**: Divine decree means humans aren't responsible, OR human responsibility means God doesn't decree (this is what we reject)

**Scripture presents sovereignty and responsibility as simultaneously true without philosophical resolution**:
- Acts 2:23: "delivered up according to the definite plan... you crucified" (both stated, no explanation)
- Luke 22:22: "goes as it has been determined, but woe to that man" (both affirmed, tension unresolved)
- Philippians 2:12-13: "work out... for God works in you" (both commanded/stated, no mechanism provided)

**The Compatibilist position**: We affirm both because Scripture affirms both. We don't require a philosophical system that makes the coexistence comprehensible to finite minds. The alternative (importing libertarian categories to "solve" the mystery) actually contradicts clear biblical teaching about sovereignty. Better to bow before revealed mystery than to resolve it by denying half the biblical witness.

**This is not intellectual surrender**; it's theological fidelity. We trust that the God who reveals Himself truly has not revealed Himself contradictorily, even if He has not revealed Himself exhaustively. The mystery remains, but it is the mystery of God's infinite wisdom, not a contradiction in His nature.

## 7. The Conclusion: The Charge Is Reversed

**The Molinist indictment of Compatibilism:**
- Assumes libertarian freedom without proving it from Scripture (burden of proof violation)
- Imposes philosophical categories ("ultimate sourcehood," "could do otherwise") foreign to biblical teaching on responsibility
- Creates a false dilemma (libertarian freedom or God authors evil) not found in Scripture
- Results in a god whose will is defeasible, whose knowledge is dependent, and whose purposes can be finally thwarted

**The actual indictment runs the other direction:**

Molinism sacrifices biblical sovereignty on the altar of philosophical intuitions about freedom. It constructs an elaborate metaphysical system (middle knowledge, feasible worlds, libertarian agency) to solve a problem Scripture never recognizes as needing solution, namely, the compatibility of divine decree and human responsibility. Scripture simply **asserts both** without explanation and judges objections to God's sovereignty as presumption (Romans 9:20).

When forced to choose between:
- **(A)** Affirming everything Scripture clearly teaches (exhaustive divine sovereignty, genuine human responsibility, divine simplicity, indefeasible divine will)
- **(B)** Modifying Scripture's teaching to fit philosophical intuitions about fairness and freedom

Molinism chooses (B). That is the fundamental error. The system is philosophy masquerading as exegesis, an attempt to make God's ways fit human categories of justice rather than submitting human categories to divine revelation.

**The proper question is not** "How can Compatibilism avoid making God the author of evil?"

**The proper question is** "Does Scripture teach both exhaustive divine sovereignty and genuine human responsibility?"

The answer is yes. Therefore, they are compatible, whether we can explain the mechanism or not. Our inability to resolve the apparent tension philosophically doesn't give us license to deny either pole of the biblical witness. Molinism denies divine sovereignty (making it defeasible by creaturely choice) to preserve an extrabiblical definition of freedom.

### This Is Idolatry

Let us be clear: **The god of Molinism is not YHWH.** It is a philosophically constructed substitute, an idol fashioned to satisfy human intuitions about autonomy, fairness, and the boundaries of divine action.

#### The Biblical YHWH:

- Works all things according to the counsel of His will, without exception (Ephesians 1:11)
- Has purposes that cannot be thwarted by any creature (Job 42:2, Isaiah 14:27)
- Accomplishes all His pleasure, and His counsel stands forever (Isaiah 46:10, Psalm 33:11)
- Has a hand that none can stay or call to account (Daniel 4:35)
- Is simple, indivisible, self-existent, dependent on nothing outside Himself (Exodus 3:14, Acts 17:25)
- Makes vessels for honorable and dishonorable use according to His sovereign right (Romans 9:21)
- Hardens whom He wills and has mercy on whom He wills (Romans 9:18)

#### The Molinist God:

- Cannot actualize certain worlds because creatures would freely reject His purposes in them
- Has saving intentions that can be finally defeated if no feasible world contains someone's free acceptance
- Possesses knowledge dependent on counterfactual facts external to His decree
- Is composite: His knowledge and will are separable, with middle knowledge logically prior to His creative decree
- Is constrained by libertarian freedom, which sets boundaries on what He can accomplish
- Must work within the limitations of what creatures would freely do
- Cannot guarantee the salvation of anyone who would reject Him in all feasible worlds

**These are not the same being.** One is sovereign over all creaturely choice; the other is subject to it. One is simple and self-sufficient; the other is composite and dependent. One's purposes are indefeasible; the other's can be thwarted. One is revealed in Scripture; the other is constructed in philosophy seminars.

The Molinist may protest: "But we affirm God's sovereignty! He chooses which feasible world to actualize!" This is theological sleight of hand. **Sovereignty constrained by creaturely libertarian freedom is not sovereignty**, it is coordination. The god who must negotiate with the autonomous wills of his creatures, who cannot guarantee the salvation of anyone who would reject him in all feasible worlds, who cannot make his saving purposes effective without creaturely cooperation that he cannot ensure, **this is not the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. This is not the God who hardened Pharaoh's heart, who gave Judas over to betrayal, who works all things after the counsel of His own will.**

**This is Baal with a Christian vocabulary.** It is a deity constructed to meet philosophical specifications, baptized with biblical language, and offered as a substitute for the God who reveals Himself in Scripture as utterly sovereign, completely self-sufficient, and absolutely free to accomplish all His purposes without dependence on creaturely choice.

### The Pattern of Idolatry

This is the ancient error in modern dress. Israel did not abandon YHWH for nothing. They exchanged Him for gods who made sense to them, gods who fit their intuitions, gods whose ways they could comprehend and control:

- **The golden calf** (Exodus 32): A god they could see and understand, not the invisible sovereign who dwelt in unapproachable light
- **Baal** (1 Kings 18): A god whose favor could be manipulated through ritual, not the God whose decrees are immutable
- **The Queen of Heaven** (Jeremiah 44): A goddess who promised prosperity without demanding absolute submission

The common thread: **Humans trading the incomprehensible God of Scripture for a deity whose ways align with human reason.** That is precisely what Molinism does. It trades:

- The God whose ways are past finding out (Romans 11:33) for a god whose decision-making process we can map in logical diagrams
- The God who makes vessels for honorable and dishonorable use according to His will (Romans 9:21) for a god who must respect libertarian autonomy
- The God who declares the end from the beginning (Isaiah 46:10) for a god who must work within the constraints of what creatures would freely do
- The God who is light with no darkness at all (1 John 1:5) for a god who must be defended against charges of moral culpability by limiting his sovereignty

**The motivation is the same in every age: to make God more palatable, more comprehensible, more acceptable to human standards of fairness and justice.** But YHWH does not submit to human auditing. He does not answer to our intuitions. He is not on trial before the bar of our philosophical preferences.

### The Severity of the Error

This is not a minor theological dispute over adiaphora. This is not a secondary issue where godly people may disagree. **This strikes at the nature of God Himself.**

When a system requires:
- **Rejecting divine simplicity** (God must be composite for middle knowledge to work)
- **Making God's purposes defeasible** (He cannot save those who would reject Him in all feasible worlds)
- **Grounding God's knowledge in facts external to His decree** (violating aseity)
- **Limiting omnipotence by libertarian categories** (God cannot make someone freely do what they would not freely do)

It has constructed a **different god**. Not a different emphasis. Not a different perspective on the same God. A different being entirely, one whose attributes are fundamentally incompatible with the God revealed in Scripture.

**Paul's words in Galatians 1:8-9 apply by extension**: "But even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed. As we have said before, so now I say again: If anyone is preaching to you a gospel contrary to the one you received, let him be accursed." If preaching a false gospel merits anathema, what does preaching a false god merit? Molinism doesn't just get soteriology wrong, it gets **theology proper** wrong. It redefines the nature, knowledge, will, and attributes of God to fit a philosophical system.

This is why the "author of evil" charge is not just wrong but **obscene in its inversion**. The system that constructs a false god (limited, composite, dependent, defeasible) has the audacity to charge those who affirm the biblical God's exhaustive sovereignty with making Him evil. It is the theological equivalent of Israel accusing Moses of abandoning YHWH while they dance around the golden calf.

### The Way Forward: Repentance and Return

The solution is not to refine Molinism, moderate it, or find a middle way. **The solution is to abandon it entirely** and return to the God who reveals Himself in Scripture:

**Epistemological humility**: Acknowledge that God's ways are higher than our ways (Isaiah 55:8-9), that His judgments are unsearchable and His ways inscrutable (Romans 11:33). We do not get to impose conditions on how sovereignty and responsibility must work in order to be acceptable to us.

**Submission to revealed paradox**: Affirm both divine sovereignty and human responsibility **because Scripture affirms both**, without demanding a philosophical mechanism that makes sense to finite minds. The tension is not a problem to be solved with middle knowledge. It is a mystery to be worshiped before.

**Trust in divine character**: The God who ordains all things is the same God who is light (1 John 1:5), who cannot be tempted with evil (James 1:13), whose eyes are too pure to look on wrong (Habakkuk 1:13). His ordination of all things does not make Him evil **because His nature is incompatible with evil**. We trust His character even where we cannot trace His ways.

**Rejection of philosophical idolatry**: Recognize that libertarian freedom is not a biblical category required for responsibility, that "ultimate sourcehood" is not the biblical criterion for culpability, and that our intuitions about fairness do not set the boundaries of divine action. These are philosophical imports, not biblical givens.

### The Final Word: Who Is Your God?

The biblical answer to the problem of divine sovereignty and human responsibility is not Molinism. It is **Genesis 18:25 combined with Romans 9:20**:

- **Genesis 18:25**: "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do what is just?" Trust His character even where you cannot comprehend His ways
- **Romans 9:20**: "But who are you, O man, to answer back to God? Will what is molded say to its molder, 'Why have you made me like this?'" Submit to His sovereignty without demanding He meet your standards of fairness

That's not evasion. That's the posture of the creature before the Creator. It is Job falling on his face after demanding an explanation: "I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you; therefore I despise myself, and repent in dust and ashes" (Job 42:5-6).

**Molinism refuses this posture.** It demands that God's sovereignty be explicable, that His ways be comprehensible, that His justice meet libertarian standards. It constructs a system where God's decree can be thwarted, His knowledge depends on external facts, and His purposes can be finally defeated. **And then it has the temerity to call this biblical.**

**It is not.** It is an idol, philosophically sophisticated, exegetically dressed up, but an idol nonetheless. A god made in the image of human intuitions about autonomy and fairness, offered as a substitute for the terrifying, incomprehensible, absolutely sovereign YHWH who does all that He pleases and gives account to no one.

### The Choice Is Binary

- **The God of Scripture**: Exhaustively sovereign, simple, self-sufficient, whose purposes cannot fail and whose knowledge depends on nothing outside Himself
- **The god of Molinism**: Coordinatively sovereign, composite, dependent on creaturely counterfactuals, whose purposes can be thwarted by libertarian choice

**These cannot both be true.** You cannot worship both. One is YHWH. The other is Baal wearing a theological mask.

**Choose this day whom you will serve.** But as for those who tremble at God's word, who submit to His revealed character, who affirm both sovereignty and responsibility because Scripture affirms both, they will worship the God who works all things according to the counsel of His will, who accomplishes all His purpose, and before whom every knee will bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

Not because creatures freely chose it in a feasible world.

But because **God decreed it, and what He decrees, He accomplishes.**

### Final Verdict

The theological prosecution is complete. Molinism's charge that Compatibilism makes God the author of evil has been examined and found to be a projection. The system making the accusation is itself guilty of constructing a false god. The logical closure is absolute:

**Molinism's god knows by dependence, wills by constraint, and reigns by permission.**

Such a being is not sovereign. Such a being is not simple. Such a being is not YHWH.

The biblical God knows by His own infinite nature, wills by His own eternal counsel, and reigns by absolute right. His purposes cannot be thwarted (Job 42:2). His hand cannot be stayed (Daniel 4:35). His counsel stands forever (Psalm 33:11). He works all things, **all things**, according to the counsel of His will (Ephesians 1:11).

This is not Molinism's god. This is the God of Scripture. And He alone deserves our worship, our submission, and our trembling adoration.

*Soli Deo Gloria.*
